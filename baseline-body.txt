Emergencies
Prepare before a crisis. In immediate danger, call Triple Zero (000) and follow the operator; these brief entries do not replace practical first-aid training.
Call Triple Zero without delaying
Call 000 for a serious, urgent threat to life or safety and request ambulance, police or fire. Give your location first, describe what happened and stay on the line; answer questions even when help feels urgent.
A$0 emergency call; ambulance charges are separate.
Connects you with the appropriate emergency response.
Everyone in Australia; a phone needs an available network.
Make your address easy to communicate
Write your address, unit, entry instructions and a nearby landmark on a household emergency card. Show children and regular visitors where it is, and review the details after moving; on rural trips, know the property or road you are using.
A$0; a short preparation task.
Reduces confusion about where help is needed.
Households, workplaces and people travelling in unfamiliar areas.
Learn CPR with practical instruction
Book hands-on CPR training. If someone is unresponsive and not breathing normally, check for danger, call 000 and begin CPR following the operator; occasional gasps are not normal breathing, and breaths are especially important after drowning.
Course fees vary in A$; allow practice time.
Builds confidence to act before paramedics arrive.
Bystanders; this reminder is not a complete first-aid lesson.
Locate the nearest AED
Find the automated external defibrillator at work, your club or a frequently visited venue before an emergency occurs. During a suspected cardiac arrest, ask another person to fetch it while CPR continues, then attach it and follow the spoken instructions.
A$0 to locate existing equipment.
Makes an available emergency resource easier to use.
Anyone at a venue with an accessible AED.
Save the poisons advice number
Save 13 11 26 for suspected poisoning or taking the wrong medicine. Keep the container available, do not induce vomiting, and call 000 immediately for unconsciousness, breathing difficulty or serious illness rather than waiting for poisons advice.
Call charges depend on your service.
Gets advice matched to the actual substance.
Households and workplaces; prevent exposures with secure storage.
Practise a household evacuation decision
Use Red Cross RediPlan to agree who will contact whom, where you will go and how pets and access needs will be managed. Discuss an alternative destination in case the first route or household contact is unavailable during a local emergency.
A$0 planning resources; discussion and preparation time.
Reduces decisions made under pressure.
Renters and owners; follow local emergency-service directions.
Build an emergency kit from existing supplies
Use the Red Cross checklist to collect essential documents, medicines information, water and practical supplies for your household. Keep the kit accessible, tell everyone where it is and replace used or expired items; customise it for children, disability needs and pets.
A$ costs vary; begin with suitable items already owned.
Makes leaving or coping with disruption easier.
All households; local hazards determine the final kit.
Keep out of floodwater
Choose another route when a road or path is flooded, even if it is familiar or another driver has crossed. Follow emergency warnings and evacuation instructions; if trapped in a life-threatening flood situation, call 000 and describe your location.
A$0 decision; alternative travel may take longer.
Avoids hidden depth, current and road damage.
Everyone; NSW SES guidance reflects a wider safety principle.
Plan a cool refuge for extreme heat
Identify a cool place you can reach and arrange check-ins for people needing help. Move strenuous tasks to cooler times; confusion, collapse or seizures in the heat require 000, immediate cooling as directed and no drinks if swallowing is unsafe.
Preparation can cost A$0; cooling and transport vary.
Reduces exposure and supports a quicker response.
Especially older people, children, outdoor workers and medically vulnerable people.
Swim where a patrol is operating
Choose a patrolled beach, read the signs and swim between the red and yellow flags. Ask a lifesaver about conditions, supervise children actively and avoid alcohol; if someone is in trouble, alert the patrol or call 000 without attempting an unsafe rescue.
Safety information costs A$0; travel costs vary.
Adds supervision and local hazard knowledge.
All beachgoers, including confident local swimmers.
Learn Australian choking first aid
Take a first-aid course that includes infants if you care for one. If someone cannot clear a blockage by coughing or cannot breathe, call 000 and follow the operator's age-appropriate instructions; do not rely on an improvised technique remembered from television.
Course fees vary in A$; practical training time.
Prepares you for a rapidly developing emergency.
Parents, carers and anyone who prepares or shares food.
Cool burns promptly and seek advice
Remove the heat source only if safe, and cool a thermal burn with cool running water for at least 20 minutes. Do not use ice or home remedies; call 000 for a serious burn and get advice about assessment for smaller burns.
Usually low A$ cost; prompt action matters.
Limits further heat injury while arranging care.
Thermal burns; chemical and electrical injuries need specific urgent advice.
Recognise a possible stroke
Remember sudden facial drooping, arm weakness or speech difficulty as reasons to call 000 immediately. Note when symptoms began or when the person was last well, and do not wait to see whether symptoms disappear before seeking emergency help.
A$0 to learn the warning signs.
Supports urgent assessment when treatment is time-sensitive.
Everyone; stroke can occur at any age.
Take possible heart-attack symptoms seriously
Call 000 for suspected heart-attack symptoms, including concerning chest discomfort with breathlessness or feeling very unwell. Stop activity and follow the operator; do not drive yourself or wait for a routine appointment because symptoms seem unusual or come and go.
A$0 emergency call; care costs depend on coverage.
Avoids a dangerous delay in emergency assessment.
Everyone; symptoms are not always the same between people.
Keep an allergy action plan accessible
If prescribed an adrenaline injector, learn its use and keep your clinician's anaphylaxis plan with it. During suspected anaphylaxis, follow that plan, use prescribed emergency treatment promptly and call 000; ensure family, school or workplace contacts know where supplies are kept.
Medicines and training costs vary in A$.
Makes an established emergency plan easier to follow.
People with severe allergies and those supporting them.
Treat a suspected snakebite as an emergency
Call 000 after a suspected snakebite, keep the person still and follow pressure-immobilisation instructions from trained guidance or the operator. Do not wash the bite, cut it, suck it or try to catch the snake; keep yourself safe as well.
A$0 to learn; first-aid kit costs vary.
Reduces harmful improvisation while help is arranged.
Anyone outdoors; bites can be difficult to see.
Know what to do around a seizure
Protect the person from nearby hazards, time the seizure and avoid restraining them or putting anything in their mouth. Call 000 for a first seizure, one lasting over five minutes, repeated seizures without recovery, injury or breathing concerns; follow any known care plan.
A$0 preparation; individual plans may need training.
Supports safer first aid and useful information for clinicians.
Bystanders and carers; immediate safety comes first.
Keep asthma emergency instructions current
Ask your clinician for a written asthma action plan and have your inhaler technique checked. Keep prescribed reliever treatment accessible; severe breathing difficulty or an asthma attack not improving with the plan requires 000, rather than waiting to find a cheaper appointment.
Appointments and medicines may cost A$.
Helps distinguish routine management from urgent deterioration.
People with asthma and their families or carers.
Everyday health
Prioritise affordable routines and prevention matched to your life. These actions support care; they do not prescribe a universal set of tests, products or targets.
Use the UV forecast, not the temperature
Check the UV index and combine clothing, shade, a protective hat, sunglasses and sunscreen when UV reaches 3 or higher. Choose broad-spectrum, water-resistant SPF50 or SPF50+ sunscreen and follow its directions; cool or cloudy weather does not guarantee low UV.
Modest recurring A$ sunscreen cost.
Supports prevention of UV damage and skin cancer.
People outdoors; follow individual advice for photosensitivity.
Attach movement to an existing routine
Choose a manageable activity connected to something you already do, such as walking for an errand or moving during a work break. Build gradually towards the current Australian movement guidance for your age and circumstances; adjust for disability, pregnancy or illness with appropriate advice.
A$0 options; regular time and safe space.
Makes activity easier to sustain.
Adults; recommendations vary across life stages.
Include strength and balance work
Ask how to add suitable muscle-strengthening, mobility and balance activities to your week. Use an approach you can perform safely and consistently, with instruction if needed; expensive equipment is not a requirement for every useful exercise.
A$0 options; instruction may have fees.
Supports everyday movement and physical function.
Adults; adapt for injury, disability or health conditions.
Plan repeatable meals before shopping
Check the pantry and fridge before writing a short meal plan and shopping list. Use affordable foods across the five food groups, and extend suitable meals with legumes, vegetables or grains; choose recipes your household will eat and that fit your cooking facilities.
Existing grocery budget in A$; planning time.
Can reduce food waste and last-minute takeaway pressure.
Households; account for allergies and prescribed medical diets.
Keep basic dental care consistent
Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and clean between teeth using a suitable method. Ask your dental practitioner to check technique and set an appropriate review interval; persistent pain, swelling or injury needs assessment rather than waiting for the next routine visit.
Modest A$ supplies; dental fees vary.
Helps prevent decay and gum problems.
Adults; children need age-specific toothpaste and supervision advice.
Check funded adult vaccines before paying
Ask a qualified immunisation provider which vaccines are recommended for your age, health, pregnancy status and other risks. Check both the National Immunisation Program and current state offers, then confirm any visit or administration charge before booking.
Eligible vaccine may cost A$0; visit fees vary.
Can close protection gaps and avoid unnecessary spending.
Funding and recommendation criteria differ by vaccine and person.
Bring vaccination records to a review
Collect the vaccination information you already have before seeing an immunisation provider. Ask them to resolve gaps or uncertain records and explain how new vaccinations will be recorded; do not assume that a remembered childhood dose settles every adult recommendation.
A$0 preparation; review fees may apply.
Makes a vaccination conversation more useful.
Adults with incomplete records or changing health circumstances.
Check screening eligibility without delaying symptoms
Check the current bowel, breast, cervical and lung screening criteria and ask which apply to you. Screening is for people without symptoms: arrange a medical assessment for a concerning change even if you are not eligible or recently had a normal screening result.
Programme funding and appointment fees vary.
Supports appropriate early detection and follow-up.
Eligibility, intervals and individual risk differ by programme.
Protect hearing before it deteriorates
Reduce exposure to loud noise and use suitable hearing protection where noise cannot be avoided. If hearing changes affect conversations or daily life, arrange assessment; sudden hearing loss needs urgent medical attention rather than waiting for a routine hearing-aid appointment.
Protection costs vary in A$; checks may have fees.
Preserves hearing and identifies treatable problems.
People exposed to noise or noticing hearing changes.
Ask about eye care for your own risk
Discuss the right eye-check schedule for your age, symptoms and health conditions with an optometrist or doctor. Mention medicines and family history, and use recommended eye protection for hazardous tasks; seek prompt assessment for sudden vision changes rather than relying on a screen test.
A$ costs depend on service and Medicare eligibility.
Connects prevention and assessment to your actual needs.
Everyone, especially people with existing eye disease or diabetes.
Make handwashing easy at the right moments
Keep soap available and wash hands after using the toilet and before preparing or eating food. Build the habit into everyday transitions, and show children the routine; cleaning products and fragrances are not a substitute for effective washing.
Low recurring A$ supply cost; brief daily effort.
Reduces transmission through contaminated hands.
Households and shared settings; follow workplace infection-control requirements.
Separate raw food from ready-to-eat food
Plan food preparation so raw meat and its juices do not contaminate other ingredients, utensils or surfaces. Follow safe cooking, storage and reheating advice, and check use-by dates; a meal-prep routine is only useful when food remains safe to eat.
Usually little extra A$ cost; kitchen organisation.
Helps reduce foodborne illness.
Home cooks; extra care for vulnerable household members.
Keep water accessible and individualise fluids
Carry or place drinking water where it is easy to reach during normal activities, especially in hot conditions. Seek advice when illness makes drinking difficult or dehydration is suspected; follow any clinician-set fluid restriction rather than adopting a universal litre target.
Tap water is low cost; a reusable bottle is optional.
Makes routine hydration easier.
Most people; infants and certain medical conditions need specific guidance.
Remove everyday trip hazards
Check frequently used paths for loose rugs, cords, clutter and poor lighting. If you have fallen, feel unsteady or fear falling, ask a health professional about balance, medicines, vision and a tailored prevention plan rather than simply restricting all activity.
Many changes cost A$0; equipment may cost more.
Reduces hazards while preserving independence.
Especially older people and anyone with balance difficulties.
Use support to quit smoking or vaping
Ask a GP, pharmacist or Quitline about a quit approach that suits your nicotine use and health. Plan for triggers and follow-up rather than treating a lapse as failure; check which support and medicines may be suitable or subsidised.
Support may be A$0; treatment costs vary.
Improves the chance of a supported, sustainable quit attempt.
People who smoke or vape; individual advice matters.
Make sexual health part of routine care
Ask a GP or sexual health clinic about protection, STI testing and contraception suited to your circumstances. Explain relevant exposure and symptoms honestly; contraception and protection against infection are separate needs, and consent remains necessary every time.
Clinic, test and product costs vary in A$.
Supports informed choices and timely care.
Sexually active people; services and confidentiality rules can vary.
Freeze a useful portion instead of forgetting it
When cooking a suitable meal, set aside a portion for a planned future lunch or dinner and store it safely. Label it clearly and put it into the coming week's plan; freezing helps only when the food is actually used.
Mostly existing food spending; freezer space and energy.
Can reduce waste and duplicate purchases.
Households with suitable storage; follow food-safety guidance.
Bring family health history to prevention discussions
Write down significant conditions in close relatives and when they developed, if known. Discuss these with your GP alongside your own concerns before purchasing a package of tests; ask what each proposed test would change.
A$0 preparation; consultation and test fees vary.
Makes prevention decisions more relevant.
Adults; missing family history should not prevent care.
Healthcare and medicines
Understand the service, the bill and the next step. Medicare, PBS and state programmes have different eligibility and cost rules.
Confirm the appointment gap before booking
Ask whether your particular service will be bulk billed and whether that applies to you. Otherwise request the total fee, expected rebate and amount payable on the day; mention any concession card and keep the invoice for checking your claim.
A$0 to ask; appointment costs vary.
Reduces surprise out-of-pocket charges.
Medicare patients; not every service or provider offers bulk billing.
Register eligible family members for Medicare Safety Nets
Check whether you should register as a couple or family so eligible medical costs can be combined. Sharing a Medicare card does not register a Safety Net family; keep details current and respond to requests to confirm family members.
A$0 registration; occasional administration.
May bring higher eligible benefits forward.
Medicare families and couples; definitions and thresholds apply.
Track the separate PBS Safety Net
Ask your pharmacist how to record eligible medicine spending and combine eligible family costs for the PBS Safety Net. Keep records across pharmacies and check the current application process; it is a separate scheme from Medicare Safety Nets for medical services.
A$0 administration; medicine costs vary.
Helps access cheaper eligible medicines after the threshold.
PBS users; annual thresholds and eligible-item rules apply.
Ask whether a generic medicine suits you
Ask the pharmacist whether a lower-cost interchangeable generic is appropriate for your prescription. Mention previous reactions or allergies because inactive ingredients and appearance can differ; confirm the medicine's identity so a packaging change does not lead to accidental duplication.
Actual A$ prices and potential savings vary.
May reduce costs while maintaining intended treatment.
Prescription users; pharmacist and prescriber restrictions still apply.
Discuss a 60-day prescription for stable treatment
Ask your prescriber whether an eligible medicine and stable ongoing condition make a 60-day prescription suitable. A new prescription and clinical assessment are required; a larger supply changes collection frequency, not the dose you should take.
A$ savings vary by medicine and funding circumstances.
May reduce dispensing expense and pharmacy trips.
Eligible medicines and suitable patients; not a universal entitlement.
Request help with a complicated medicine routine
Bring prescription medicines, non-prescription products and supplements into a conversation with your GP or pharmacist. Ask whether a medicines review would help, and clarify interactions or confusing instructions before making changes yourself; confirm any referral and funding requirements.
Review and consultation fees vary in A$.
Can simplify understanding and reduce medicine errors.
People using multiple medicines or struggling with their routine.
Return unwanted medicines to a pharmacy
Check expiry dates and separate medicines you no longer need for return to a community pharmacy. Do not put them in household rubbish or down the drain; arrange replacement essential supplies before discarding them and ask about special disposal for sharps.
Medicine returns are generally A$0; sharps arrangements vary.
Reduces unsafe storage and environmental contamination.
All households with medicines, including over-the-counter products.
Check ambulance cover when circumstances change
Review state-funded coverage, concessions, subscriptions or insurance before moving or travelling interstate. Ask about treatment without transport, air ambulance and exclusions; Medicare does not cover ambulance, but an emergency still requires calling 000 immediately rather than investigating costs.
A$0 review; premiums and bills vary.
Finds gaps or duplicate cover before a trip.
Australian residents; coverage depends on location and circumstances.
Take a short appointment agenda
Write your main concern, its timeline and the decision you need help making. Bring relevant results and ask your most important question early; use healthdirect's Question Builder if you find it difficult to organise what to say.
A$0 preparation; a few minutes.
Uses limited consultation time more effectively.
All patients; arrange appropriate appointment length and access support.
Choose a regular GP you can work with
Look for a GP whose availability, communication, access and fees fit your needs, then arrange transfer of relevant records when changing practices. An ongoing relationship can help coordinate care; ask how urgent concerns and after-hours needs are handled locally.
Fees vary in A$; initial search time.
Supports continuity across separate health issues.
People seeking ongoing care, including in regional areas.
Find urgent care before the need arises
Locate your nearest Medicare Urgent Care Clinic and check its hours. These clinics handle urgent problems that are not life-threatening when timely GP care is unavailable; for a life-threatening illness or injury, call 000 or attend an emergency department instead.
Bulk-billed eligible care; transport costs may apply.

Check a specialist referral's practical details

A$0 enquiries; specialist and travel costs vary.
Prevents avoidable booking and cost surprises.
Patients referred for specialist or allied healthcare.
Agree who will follow up results

A$0 reminder; follow-up consultation fees may apply.
Reduces uncertainty after testing.
Anyone having a test, referral or treatment review.
Carry an accurate medicine list
Keep a list of medicines, supplements, allergies and the directions currently agreed with your clinician. Update it after changes and bring it when seeing a new provider; include products bought without a prescription rather than assuming they cannot interact.

Improves information shared between healthcare providers.

Ask for access to your health information
Request relevant records or a summary when moving providers, and ask how corrections to factual errors can be made. Check the service's identification, consent and fee process; keep your own copies securely and share them only with people who need them.
Access fees and processing times may vary.

Seek another opinion before a major decision
If a proposed non-urgent treatment leaves you uncertain, ask about obtaining another qualified opinion and what information should accompany it. Compare the reasons, likely outcomes and alternatives, including waiting; do not postpone emergency treatment while pursuing extra opinions.
Consultation and record-transfer costs may apply.

Patients considering significant treatment where time permits.
Ask for an explanation you can use
Before consenting, ask the clinician to explain the purpose, material risks, alternatives and expected costs in plain language. Tell them what matters to you and request communication support when needed; understanding the decision matters more than silently agreeing to unfamiliar terms.

Use healthcare feedback channels when care falls short

Mental health and energy
Professional care, rest, safer work and connection can all matter. Seek support that fits your circumstances, and use emergency services for immediate danger.
Save crisis support before you need it
For immediate danger, call 000. For distress or a personal crisis, Lifeline provides confidential support 24/7 by phone on 13 11 14, text on 0477 13 11 14 or online chat; save the option you are most likely to use.
Lifeline support costs A$0; connection required.
Makes human support easier to reach.
People in Australia, including those worried about someone else.
Help someone connect with crisis support
If someone tells you they are struggling, listen without judgement and offer to stay with them or remain connected while they seek help. Use Lifeline for advice about supporting another person, and call 000 when their life is in immediate danger.
A$0 support service; time and attention.
Provides company during the next step towards help.
Friends and family; keep your own safety in mind.
Find a free mental health starting point
Check the location and opening hours of a Medicare Mental Health Centre. Centres offer free support without a referral, appointment or Mental Health Treatment Plan; they can help connect you with further care but are not an emergency crisis service.
A$0 centre services; transport may cost extra.
Provides an accessible first conversation about support.
Local availability varies; use 000 for immediate danger.
Ask about a Mental Health Treatment Plan

Eligible patients; benefit limits and referral rules apply.
Protect a consistent sleep opportunity

Ask about treatment for persistent insomnia

Treatment costs and availability vary in A$.

Adults with persistent sleep difficulty; never change prescribed medicines alone.

Everyday costs

 A practical decision filter

Before spending time or money, ask:

- Is this the problem I actually need to solve?
- What happens if I wait, and is there a real deadline?
- What is the full cost, including upkeep, travel and my time?
- Does this rule or service apply to my state, circumstances and date?
- What would a small, reversible first step look like?

 Editorial policy

This guide helps readers find a sensible next action. It is a broad educational resource for people living in Australia, including citizens and residents with different eligibility conditions, locations, abilities, incomes and family situations.

 What the labels mean

Official rule identifies a requirement, entitlement or process explained by the authority responsible for it. A summary can leave out exceptions; the linked official source and the reader's circumstances remain decisive.

Official / professional guidance identifies guidance from a government agency, regulator, recognised professional organisation or relevant primary service provider. It does not mean that every sentence is a research result, that the evidence is equally strong across topics, or that an organisation endorses this guide.

Practical suggestion identifies editorial judgement: a planning method, question to ask or organisational habit. These entries do not claim measured benefits or a universal best choice. A source may be linked for context without converting the whole suggestion into an official recommendation.

The action is the central instruction. Cost / effort and Potential value are qualitative editorial assessments, not quotations, measured savings or promised outcomes. Applies to is a scope cue, not an eligibility determination.

 Review dates and change

This first edition was prepared and editorially reviewed on 9 October 2026. Linked sources were consulted during preparation; review used readable official pages, documents and, where direct access was limited, indexed primary-source text. A date does not certify every linked page's ongoing availability or every possible exception. Practical entries carry an editorial review date, not an evidence-verification date.

Check the live source before acting on a deadline, benefit, price, legal process, medical recommendation or eligibility rule. Program names, terms, provider fees and state legislation can change. Federal guidance does not automatically settle a state or territory question. A link that loads successfully does not, by itself, validate a claim.

The repository does not run an automatic policy-monitoring service. Future editions should prioritise urgent errors, changed rules and broken primary links, then improve coverage and clarity. Substantial corrections belong in the changelog.

 Health, law and money

Individual circumstances matter. The guide does not diagnose conditions, select treatment, assess a legal case, determine entitlement, calculate a person's tax liability or recommend an investment product. Use it to prepare questions and find the appropriate professional or official pathway. In immediate danger call 000.

Avoid turning a broad recommendation into a universal screening age, medication dose, rebate promise or legal deadline. When a precise rule is necessary, identify the jurisdiction, effective date, scope and authoritative source.

 Source and writing standards

- Prefer the agency administering the rule, a regulator, a recognised professional body or the service provider responsible for the pathway.
- Link to the page supporting the claim. Use a landing page only when the next step is intentionally to find local details there.
- Use Australian English and concrete actions. Explain acronyms when they may be unfamiliar.
- Avoid numerical savings, health-effect claims, comparative rankings and guarantees without suitable evidence and context.
- Keep lived experience and practical advice welcome, while labelling them honestly.
- Do not add affiliate links, sponsored rankings, referral codes or invented testimonials.
- Preserve upstream attribution and third-party rights. Rephrase accurately and quote sparingly.

 Preparation and limitations

This edition was prepared with AI assistance, source consultation, editorial checks and automated structural validation. It has not been independently reviewed by medical, legal or financial professionals. The number of entries measures coverage, not scientific quality or the number of distinct research findings.

The project adapts the original guide at the level of themes. It does not reproduce every original recommendation. Readers can inspect the source map, source directory and attribution, and propose corrections through the repository.
